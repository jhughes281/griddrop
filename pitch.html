Concept pitch · Built for everywhere — piloting in Houston

The last 500 feet is the hardest part of every delivery.

GPS gets a driver to the complex. Then it quits. The driver is left circling buildings that aren't numbered in order, guessing which floor unit 1107 is on, and stuck at a gate with no code. Multiply that by every driver, every carrier, every day — at every apartment complex in America. GridDrop works anywhere there are apartments; Houston is where the network starts, because that's where our founder drives.

Why this exists

GridDrop was designed by a working delivery driver. The wasted minutes, the misdelivered packages, the gate you can't get through while the customer's ice cream melts — this app is that experience, turned into a map. One driver figures a complex out once; every driver after them skips the hunt.

01What it does

GridDrop is a crowd-built delivery map of apartment complexes. For each complex in the network, a driver gets three things before they're off the main road:

PIN Unit → building + floor

Type the unit numbers on your run. Each one pins to its building on a site map and tells you the floor — because unit numbers almost never do.

ACCESS Before you pull in

Gate code, door-lock schedule, drop-off policy, office hours — the info that decides whether a stop takes 90 seconds or 15 minutes.

GUIDE Stop-by-stop

Guided run through your stops: which stairwell, park nose-out, hazards on. Written the way drivers actually talk.

02The network effect

Every complex only has to be figured out once. A driver maps it in five minutes — snap the site-map sign at the entrance, confirm the building layout, label the unit ranges and floors, add the one tip they'd tell the next driver. From then on, every courier from every carrier who pulls in benefits. Coverage compounds: the map gets better every time someone drives. And because drivers are everywhere apartments are, the network scales city by city — the playbook that wins Houston wins Dallas, Atlanta, and Phoenix the same way.

Cold start is the classic objection, and it has a Houston answer: the founder's own daily routes seed the first complexes, and sister company Zen Lockers (smart lockers, vending, and valet trash for multifamily properties) brings property-manager relationships that turn mapped complexes into sanctioned ones.

04How it makes money

B2B Fleet subscriptions

Amazon DSPs, courier fleets, and last-mile contractors pay per seat for team routes, hand-off, and analytics. New-driver ramp time is their biggest hidden cost — GridDrop attacks it directly.

B2B Property listings+

Claiming is free. Premium listings add delivery instructions, locker/parcel-room integration, and analytics — a natural bundle with on-site amenity services like Zen Lockers.

B2C Driver Pro

Free forever for the basics. Pro adds multi-stop optimization across complexes, offline maps, and shift stats for gig drivers who live in three different apps a day.

05Trust & safety — the part that matters

Access info is a privilege, not a scrape

  • Verified couriers only. Gate codes and access notes are never public — visible only to identity-verified, actively delivering couriers.
  • Properties hold the keys. A claimed complex controls its own listing: what's shown, to whom, and codes can be rotated or pulled instantly.
  • Sanctioned beats scraped. The goal is property-authorized access profiles — the version of this data managers want to exist, because it's the alternative to codes being texted around uncontrolled.
  • Audit trail. Every access-info view is logged. Abuse gets an account banned and a property notified.
